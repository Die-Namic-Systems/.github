# Die-Namic-Systems

### Everything worth keeping tends to decay. This is the toolkit that fights back.

> A dynamic system's failure mode is drift — it changes, quietly, until it no longer means what it meant. Die-Namic-Systems holds the verification layer and the shared instincts the fleet runs on: local-first, human-governed, and honest over time by design.

Memory rots. Links go dead. Models drift. Records get decommissioned and data leaks. These look like four different problems; they're one — **loss** — wearing four coats. **[Nestor](https://github.com/Die-Namic-Systems/Nestor)** answers the question every face needs answered: *has a human checked this?* The sibling orgs each fight loss in their own domain.

---

## The principle

**Local-first.** Your data lives on hardware you control, in formats you can read and delete. Cloud optional. Amnesia discouraged.

**Human-governed.** The machine proposes; the human ratifies. Consent is explicit and expires with the session. Nothing consequential happens on the software's own authority.

**Durable / drift-resistant.** Version the truth, monitor it automatically, and surface decay loudly. A record that survives is one that knows when it's slipping.

## What we won't do

We won't make you sign in to use your own tools. We won't move your data to "improve the product." We won't let software act without a human turning the second key. We won't ship what we can't audit, delete, or explain.

**Tools, not traps.**
